# The large deployed mesh: audio, simulated listeners

On 22 November the owner asked to "test on a larger deployed mesh", "with
simulated users too - as many as we can simulate. lets stick to audio for
now", and then raised the budget to about $60 to $70 in all, the
watchdog's cap included. The topology starts from the referee's plan
(needletail-compare `target/compare/scale-plan.md`) and is grown to as many
listener hosts as that budget buys, with enough edges in each region that
on the second curve the edges, not the listener hosts, give out first. The
topology file is `deploy/low-latency/topology-scale.json`, generated by
`scripts/scale_topology.py`. The run's driver is `scripts/compare/scale-run.py`.

## What is measured

- **Audio only.** Three lanes, one per ramp, all from the AEP1 sender on
  lon-src through the contributor:
  - `pcm-1`: 1 ms 24-bit PCM, stream 4001
  - `opus-1`: 1 ms Opus Custom at 320 kb/s, stream 3001
  - `opus-2.5`: standard 2.5 ms Opus, stream 3001
- **Listener steps:** 100, 1,000, 5,000, 10,000, 20,000 and up (about
  28,000 UDP listeners of 1 ms PCM fill the second curve's edges). Each
  step is spread over the regions in proportion to their edges, and over a
  region's listener hosts in proportion to their vCPUs. Half of each step
  listens over UDP and half over LL-HLS (`--hls-share 0` for UDP alone):
  - **UDP:** `needletail-load` from av-mesh branch `scale-load`. Each
    listener has its own socket and subscription, with the real FEC and
    compact decode, NACKs and loss accounting. Results are sampled rather
    than logged, as histograms of publication-to-arrival time and of time
    to first part.
  - **LL-HLS:** `ntcmp hls --count` with 40 part requests in flight over
    HTTP/3. Readers are pinned evenly to the region's edges (`cache=`), or
    left to the region's steering (`--hls-steering steered`). ntcmp
    measures mouth to ear: the age of each part against the source's spoken
    time.
- **At every step**, per region: p50 and p99 of each lane, the share of
  parts missing, and the time to first part or first audio. Also UDP
  listeners per edge, plus CPU, bytes and packets out of every node of
  every tier, from a 1 s sampler on all nodes. That data shows whether the
  contributor and forwarder tiers stay flat as listeners grow.
- **Which tier gave out first, and when:** an edge, a forwarder, the
  contributor or a listener host. A node gives out when its CPU stays at
  90% or more for 3 s, or its bytes out stay at 90% of its plan's outbound
  cap. An edge also gives out when its UDP listeners miss more than 1% of
  an interval's parts, counted only from the listener hosts that kept up
  in that interval. A listener host gives out on CPU or UDP receive-buffer
  drops held for 3 s, or on needletail-load's own verdict held for 3
  intervals (host CPU or one busy core, worker busy time, late timers,
  receive lag, socket drops, or drops before the sockets at the NIC and
  backlog). When a listener host saturates, the step is stopped at once.
  From then on the figures would describe the load host, not the mesh.
- **The ramp ends** at the first step where something gave out
  (`--keep-going` runs past it).
- **Moving the curve out:** two edges per region, then five (curve 1),
  then ten (curve 2). The standby setting switches edges in and out with a
  restart, so no instances are created mid-run.
- **Control:** self-hosted LiveKit at a listener count it can carry, about
  450 per 2-vCPU SFU on wave 2. The referee runs it with its own tools.

Prices were read from `GET /linode/types` on 22 November: $0.054, $0.108
and $0.216 an hour for g6-dedicated-2, -4 and -8, with no region override
in gb-lon, us-east, ap-northeast or ap-southeast. Outbound caps are 4, 5
and 6 Gbit/s. Public transfer past the account's pool costs $0.005/GB
(`GET /network-transfer/prices`). `scripts/linode-small-mesh-lab.py check
--topology deploy/low-latency/topology-scale.json` re-reads all of this
from the API's public endpoints, plus each region's capacity for each
plan. It needs no token and creates nothing.

**Capacity and account limits.** `GET /regions/<id>/availability` on 22
November (re-read for this sizing): g6-dedicated-2, -4 and -8 available in
gb-lon, us-east and ap-northeast; in ap-southeast g6-dedicated-2 and -4
only. Sydney's listener hosts are therefore sixteen g6-dedicated-4, the
same 64 vCPUs as the other regions' eight g6-dedicated-8 at the same
price. The availability endpoint has been wrong for Sydney before, which
is why Sydney is optional and created first. What this account may create
in all (instances, dedicated vCPUs: 40 g6-dedicated-2, 22 g6-dedicated-4
and 24 g6-dedicated-8 are 344 vCPUs) is not in a public endpoint and was
not checked: confirm it before `up` (Linode support raises the limits on
request). If the account refuses an instance, `up` deletes the partial
lab; every instance created bills a full hour, so a refused `up` costs up
to about $10.

**Listeners on the VLAN.** The listener and view hosts are on their
region's VLAN (.100 the view host, .101 up the listener hosts), and every
listener reads the edges there (`playback_address=vlan`). Linode does not
bill VLAN traffic, but it bills public egress. One 1 ms PCM UDP listener
is about 5.6 Mbit/s on the wire, so 28,000 of them are about 157 Gbit/s:
some 70 TB an hour, or about $350 an hour past the pool. `scale-run.py
run` refuses to start when a listener host or an edge has no VLAN address,
or when the deploy's `playback_address` is not `vlan`. Public traffic that
remains: the long-haul forwarder links (one stream to six remote
forwarders, a few Mbit/s) and the LiveKit control. Both fit in the pool.
Every step reports the public bytes that left each node
(`public_transfer_gb`).

Edges 6 to 10 of every region are created with the lab but held out of the
first curve. The topology's `standby_edges`, passed to the deploy as the
setting of the same name, runs no service on them, and no distributor
peers or steers to them. The second curve restarts them in. This avoids
creating instances in the middle of a run.

Sydney is optional. `up --optional-sites sydney` creates Sydney's 30 nodes
first. If one of them cannot be created, it deletes them and goes on
without Sydney, which saves $2.538 an hour. Every tool works without it.
`--without-sites sydney` leaves Sydney out from the start.

## Cost and duration

Each step takes its ramp (listeners join over 10 to 60 s), a 60 s hold
that is measured, and about 75 s to start, drain, fetch and settle: about
2.5 to 3 minutes. A ramp of six steps takes about 15 minutes. To see the
current figures, run `scale-run.py plan --topology-only`.

| Phase | Minutes |
| --- | ---: |
| `up`, bootstrap and `push` of 99 nodes, LiveKit, `prepare` | 75 |
| Calibration ramp (100, 1,000, 8,000, mixed), then `plan --cost` | 10 |
| Two edges per region: pcm-1 UDP-only ramp to the edges' limit | 15 |
| Curve 1, five edges per region: pcm-1 UDP-only and mixed, opus-1 and opus-2.5 mixed, opus-2.5 UDP-only | 60 |
| Curve 2, ten edges per region: pcm-1 and opus-2.5 UDP-only, opus-2.5 mixed | 45 |
| Self-hosted LiveKit control | 20 |
| Reruns of steps that failed for reasons other than load | 30 |
| `down`, VLAN and leftover check | 10 |
| **Total** | **265, about 4.4 h** |

At $10.422 an hour, 4.4 hours is about $46. **The watchdog is armed for 6
hours by `up --watchdog-hours 6`, before the first instance is created,
which caps the lab at 6 x $10.422 = $62.53**, plus well under $1 of public
transfer: the worst case stays under $70. The 1.6 hours between the
schedule and the cap are the margin for one rerun of a curve's ramps. Do
not move the watchdog past 6.5 hours ($67.74). Without Sydney the same
6 hours cap the lab at $47.30. Listener traffic stays on the VLAN and is
not billed; `scale-run.py plan` prints what it would cost if it were
public.

## Expectations (to be confirmed by the run)

- **At 1 ms, the listener hosts carry about 32,000 UDP listeners.**
  needletail-load's bench (part L, 22 Nov) measured 3.96 µs of CPU per
  datagram, kernel included, with nothing missing. Adding about 1.5 µs of
  NIC receive softirq gives 5.5 µs, or 0.0055 vCPU per listener of a
  1,000-part stream: about 1,000 listeners per g6-dedicated-8 within 70%
  busy, 8,000 per region of 64 vCPUs, about 32,000 in four regions (24,000
  without Sydney). At 2.5 ms (opus-2.5, 400 parts a second) about 2.5
  times as many. The cost is per datagram because each listener has its
  own socket, so the spec's 5,000 per g6-dedicated-4 at 1 ms is out of
  reach. Memory is about 0.44 MB per listener and does not bind: the
  1,000 listeners of a g6-dedicated-8 take about 0.44 GB of its 16 GB.
- **An edge sends at most 4 Gbit/s,** about 700 PCM listeners at 1 ms or
  about 3,000 at 1 ms Opus. Ten edges a region send to about 7,000 PCM
  listeners at 1 ms, below the region's 8,000: on the second curve the
  edges give out first (at about 28,000 listeners in all, the listener
  hosts near 60% busy). Five edges a region (curve 1) give out at about
  14,000, two (`settings --edges-per-region 2`) at about 5,600. With
  opus-2.5 an edge sends 0.6 Mbit/s a listener, so its CPU, not its NIC,
  is what that ramp tests; the listener hosts drive about 80,000 of them.
- **LL-HLS at 1 ms parts is the load that runs out first on the listener
  hosts.** Each reader asks for 1,000 parts a second. The plan's planning
  figure is 0.03 vCPU of a listener host per reader, so a g6-dedicated-8
  carries about 190 readers within 70% busy, about 6,000 in all. For
  pcm-1, the plan marks the half LL-HLS 10,000 step as over the listener
  hosts. That is why the UDP-only ramps are in the schedule.
  `--hls-cap-per-host N` keeps LL-HLS at what the hosts carry and sends
  the rest over UDP (marked "clipped" in the plan).
- **The contributor and forwarder tiers should stay flat:** one stream in,
  and one feed per edge (ten a forwarder on curve 2) and per remote
  forwarder.
- **Every step measures what a listener cost its host.** It reports
  needletail-load's and ntcmp's own CPU, as the sampler reads it per
  process, and peak memory, both per listener, as the median over the
  listener hosts. UDP and LL-HLS each get 0.0015 vCPU of NIC softirq
  added, which a process's own CPU time does not include. The run writes
  these to `cost.json`, in the shape that `--cost FILE` takes, for
  `plan` and `run` to use. Pass `--udp-mbps` once a step has measured
  the wire rate. One measured figure is still missing: what an LL-HLS
  reader costs (the plan assumes 0.03 vCPU at 1 ms parts, part L's
  unmeasured estimate for HTTP/2; HTTP/3 may cost more). The calibration
  ramp measures it before the large steps.

   --target x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu.2.36 --bin av-mesh --bin needletail-load \

Calibration. A short mixed ramp, held for 30 s per step, measures what a
UDP listener and an LL-HLS reader cost a listener host. At 8,000 it runs

```sh

```

Each run's results go to `target/compare/scale-<name>/`:

- `plan.json`: allocation, capacity and cost
- `events.jsonl`: the monitor's lines and the stops
- `steps/<step>/<node>/`: the sampler, needletail-load's `udp/`
  (intervals, summary, the sampled listeners' arrivals) and the readers'
  condensed reports
- `steps/<step>/counters.json`: the edges' client UDP counters for the step
- `steps/<step>/step.json`
- `scale.json` and `summary.md`: per step and region, the lanes' p50 and
  p99, missing parts, time to first part, the tiers' CPU and Mbit/s out,
  UDP listeners per edge, public transfer, and what gave out first

- `cost.json`: the measured cost of a listener, for `--cost`

`scale-run.py analyse <name>` rebuilds `scale.json`, `summary.md` and
`cost.json` from the files.

## Limits of the tools

- **The LL-HLS figures are per reader.** ntcmp keeps a distribution per
  reader and no histogram. A region's p50 is the parts-weighted median of
  the readers' medians, and its p99 is the parts-weighted p99 of their
  p99s, which is an upper bound. The UDP figures merge exactly when every
  needletail-load summary carries its histogram buckets. When one does
  not, the worst host bounds the tail, and the report says so (`"merge"`).
- **ntcmp keeps every reader's latency values in memory**, one float per
  part per reader, and writes one report per reader. Each step runs a
  fresh process, which bounds that memory at these step lengths (about 3
  MB per reader for a 90 s step), but an hour-long crowd would not fit.
  The summary shows ntcmp's peak MB per reader.
- **UDP latency is publication to arrival**, the part's publish time as
  the client reads it. LL-HLS latency is mouth to ear. The view hosts'
  full probes, run with the referee's tools, give mouth to ear for both
  lanes.
- **The Opus readers' indexes number part N as epoch N of the loop with
  offset 0**, as the referee's `nt-popus-120s` does. If the AEP1 Opus lane
  shifts by the encoder's look-ahead, every Opus mouth-to-ear figure moves
  by that constant: 1 ms for Opus Custom, 2.5 ms for standard Opus.
