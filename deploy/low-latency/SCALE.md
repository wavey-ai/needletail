# The large deployed mesh: audio, simulated listeners

On 22 November the owner asked to "test on a larger deployed mesh", "with
simulated users too - as many as we can simulate. lets stick to audio for
now". This plan matches the referee's (needletail-compare
`target/compare/scale-plan.md`) node for node. It differs in two places,
and both reasons are given below. The topology file is
`deploy/low-latency/topology-scale.json`, generated by
`scripts/scale_topology.py`. The run's driver is `scripts/compare/scale-run.py`.

## What is measured

- **Audio only.** Three lanes, one per ramp, all from the AEP1 sender on
  lon-src through the contributor:
  - `pcm-1`: 1 ms 24-bit PCM, stream 4001
  - `opus-1`: 1 ms Opus Custom at 320 kb/s, stream 3001
  - `opus-2.5`: standard 2.5 ms Opus, stream 3001
- **Listener steps:** 100, 1,000, 5,000, 10,000, 20,000 and up. Each step is
  spread over the regions in proportion to their edges, and over a region's
  listener hosts in proportion to their vCPUs. Half of each step listens
  over UDP and half over LL-HLS:
  - **UDP:** `needletail-load` from av-mesh branch `scale-load`. Each
    listener has its own socket and subscription, with the real FEC and
    compact decode, NACKs and loss accounting. Results are sampled rather
    than logged, as histograms of publication-to-arrival time and of time
    to first part.
  - **LL-HLS:** `ntcmp hls --count` with 40 part requests in flight over
    HTTP/3. Readers are pinned evenly to the region's edges (`cache=`), or
    left to the region's steering (`--hls-steering steered`). ntcmp
    measures mouth to ear: the age of each part against the source's spoken
    time.
- **At every step**, per region: p50 and p99 of each lane, the share of
  parts missing, and the time to first part or first audio. Also UDP
  listeners per edge, plus CPU, bytes and packets out of every node of
  every tier, from a 1 s sampler on all nodes. That data shows whether the
  contributor and forwarder tiers stay flat as listeners grow.
- **Which tier gave out first, and when:** an edge, a forwarder, the
  contributor or a listener host. A node gives out when its CPU stays at
  90% or more for 3 s, or its bytes out stay at 90% of its plan's outbound
  cap. An edge also gives out when its UDP listeners miss more than 1% of
  an interval's parts. A listener host gives out on CPU, on UDP
  receive-buffer drops, or on needletail-load's own verdict. When a
  listener host saturates, the step is stopped at once. From then on the
  figures would describe the load host, not the mesh.
- **The ramp ends** at the first step where something gave out
  (`--keep-going` runs past it).
- **Moving the curve out:** two edges per region, then four, then London
  with eight. The standby setting switches edges in and out with a
  restart, so no instances are created mid-run. Which lane shows each
  curve depends on the listener hosts' capacity (see Expectations).
- **Control:** self-hosted LiveKit at a listener count it can carry, about
  450 per 2-vCPU SFU on wave 2. The referee runs it with its own tools.

Prices were read from `GET /linode/types` on 22 November: $0.054, $0.108
and $0.216 an hour for g6-dedicated-2, -4 and -8, with no region override
in gb-lon, us-east, ap-northeast or ap-southeast. Outbound caps are 4, 5
and 6 Gbit/s. Public transfer past the account's pool costs $0.005/GB
(`GET /network-transfer/prices`). `scripts/linode-small-mesh-lab.py check
--topology deploy/low-latency/topology-scale.json` re-reads all of this
from the API's public endpoints, plus each region's capacity for each
plan. It needs no token and creates nothing.

**Differences from the referee's plan:**

1. **Sydney's listener hosts are four g6-dedicated-4, not two
   g6-dedicated-8.** On 22 November `GET /regions/ap-southeast/availability`
   showed no g6-dedicated-8 capacity. The swap keeps the same 16 vCPUs at
   the same price. It brings the total to 49 nodes, against 47 in the
   referee's plan, for the same $4.158/h.
2. **The listener and view hosts are on their region's VLAN** (.100 and
   .101 up), and every listener reads the edges there
   (`playback_address=vlan`). Linode does not bill VLAN traffic, but it
   bills public egress. One 1 ms PCM UDP listener is about 5.6 Mbit/s on
   the wire, so 5,000 of them are 28 Gbit/s: about 12.6 TB an hour, or
   about $60 an hour past the pool. Over the public network, the 10,000
   step alone would cost more than the whole budget. `scale-run.py run`
   refuses to start when a listener host or an edge has no VLAN address,
   or when the deploy's `playback_address` is not `vlan`. Public traffic
   that remains: the long-haul forwarder links (one stream to six remote
   forwarders, a few Mbit/s) and the LiveKit control. Both fit in the
   pool. Every step reports the public bytes that left each node
   (`public_transfer_gb`).

London's edges 5 to 8 are created with the lab but held out of the first
curve. The topology's `standby_edges`, passed to the deploy as the setting
of the same name, runs no service on them, and no distributor peers or
steers to them. The second curve restarts them in. This avoids creating
instances in the middle of a run, and costs $0.216 an hour.

Sydney is optional. `up --optional-sites sydney` creates Sydney's 12 nodes
first. If one of them cannot be created, it deletes them and goes on
without Sydney, which saves $0.918 an hour. Every tool works without it.
`--without-sites sydney` leaves Sydney out from the start.

## Cost and duration

Each step takes its ramp (listeners join over 10 to 60 s), a 60 s hold
that is measured, and about 75 s to start, drain, fetch and settle: about
2.5 minutes. A ramp of five steps takes about 12 to 13 minutes. To see the
current figures, run `scale-run.py plan --topology-only`.

| Phase | Minutes |
| --- | ---: |
| `up`, bootstrap and `push` of 49 nodes, LiveKit, `prepare` | 60 |
| Two edges per region: pcm-1 UDP-only ramp to the edges' limit | 15 |
| Four edges per region: pcm-1 UDP-only and mixed, opus-1 and opus-2.5 mixed | 52 |
| Second curve: restart with eight London edges, opus-2.5 UDP-only and mixed | 31 |
| Self-hosted LiveKit control | 20 |
| Reruns of steps that failed for reasons other than load | 30 |
| `down`, VLAN and leftover check | 10 |
| **Total** | **about 3.7 h** |

At $4.158 an hour, 3.7 hours is about $15. The watchdog is armed for 5.5
hours, which caps the bill at about $23. One full rerun brings it to
about $38, under the $60 limit. Listener traffic stays on the VLAN and is
not billed. `scale-run.py plan` prints what it would cost if it were
public.

## Expectations (to be confirmed by the run)

- **LL-HLS at 1 ms parts is the load that runs out first, on both sides.**
  Each reader asks for 1,000 parts a second. The plan's planning figure is
  0.03 vCPU of a listener host per reader, so a g6-dedicated-8 carries
  about 190 readers within 70% busy. For pcm-1, the plan marks the half
  LL-HLS 5,000 step as over the listener hosts. That is why the UDP-only
  ramp is in the schedule. `--hls-cap-per-host N` keeps LL-HLS at what the
  hosts carry and sends the rest over UDP (marked "clipped" in the plan).
- **At 1 ms, the listener hosts carry about 8,000 UDP listeners.**
  needletail-load's bench (part L, 22 Nov) measured 3.96 µs of CPU per
  datagram, kernel included, with nothing missing. Adding about 1.5 µs of
  NIC receive softirq gives 5.5 µs, or 0.0055 vCPU per listener of a
  1,000-part stream. That is about 1,000 listeners per g6-dedicated-8 and
  500 per g6-dedicated-4 within 70% busy. The listener hosts have 64
  vCPUs in all, about 8,000 listeners at 1 ms (pcm-1, opus-1) and about
  20,000 at 2.5 ms (opus-2.5, 400 parts a second). The cost is per
  datagram because each listener has its own socket, so the spec's 5,000
  per g6-dedicated-4 at 1 ms is out of reach. Memory is about 0.44 MB per
  listener and does not bind. One measured figure is still missing: what
  an LL-HLS reader costs. The plan assumes 0.03 vCPU at 1 ms parts.
- **An edge sends at most 4 Gbit/s,** about 700 PCM listeners at 1 ms or
  about 3,000 at 1 ms Opus. Over 16 edges that is about 11,000 PCM
  listeners, more than the listener hosts drive at 1 ms. To see an edge
  give out at 1 ms PCM, the run uses two edges per region
  (`settings --edges-per-region 2`). Eight edges then carry about 5,000 to
  6,000 listeners while the listener hosts stay near 75% busy. Four edges
  per region then shows how far the curve moves out, up to the listener
  hosts' 8,000. With opus-2.5, the listener hosts drive about 20,000
  listeners, about 1,250 per edge on 16 edges. That is 750 Mbit/s per
  edge, so an edge's CPU, not its NIC, is what that ramp tests.
- **London with eight edges (the second curve)** cannot reach its edges'
  bandwidth at 1 ms PCM with London's two listener hosts (about 2,000
  listeners). It shows the curve moving out at opus-2.5, or at 1 ms PCM
  with about three more g6-dedicated-8 listener hosts in London
  (+$0.648/h, 52 nodes). Those hosts are not in the topology.
- **The contributor and forwarder tiers should stay flat:** one stream in,
  and one feed per edge and per remote forwarder.
- Pass better figures to `plan` and `run` with `--cost FILE`
  (`{"udp_vcpu": ..., "hls_vcpu": ...}`, vCPU per listener at 1,000 parts
  a second) and `--udp-mbps` once a step has measured them.

## How the referee runs it

Builds (Linux, x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu, as for the latency labs):

```sh
# av-mesh with needletail-load (branch scale-load), av-contrib, daw-nexus:
#   cargo zigbuild --release --target x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu.2.36
export NEEDLETAIL_AV_MESH_ROOT=/home/jamieb/wavey.ai/av-mesh-scale
export NEEDLETAIL_AV_CONTRIB_ROOT=/home/jamieb/wavey.ai/av-contrib-run   # or the referee's
cd /home/jamieb/wavey.ai/needletail-scale
scripts/compare/gobuild.sh                         # target/ntcmp (listener hosts get it)
cargo build --release --bin needletail-compile
```

The lab. The tool's default ceilings are 40 nodes and $1/h, so this
topology needs them raised on the command line:

```sh
T=deploy/low-latency/topology-scale.json
scripts/linode-small-mesh-lab.py check --topology $T     # prices, capacity; no token
export NEEDLETAIL_LINODE_MAX_NODES=49 NEEDLETAIL_LINODE_MAX_HOURLY_USD=4.2
scripts/linode-small-mesh-lab.py up --topology $T --optional-sites sydney
scripts/linode-small-mesh-lab.py watchdog --hours 5.5
scripts/linode-small-mesh-lab.py hosts                   # forwarders on distinct hosts
scripts/linode-small-mesh-deploy.py push $(scripts/compare/scale-run.py settings)
scripts/compare/compare_lab.py livekit                   # the referee's LiveKit (needs *-lk, lk-redis)
scripts/compare/scale-run.py prepare \
  --audio-file /home/jamieb/wavey.ai/needletail-compare/target/compare-media/audio/audio.s24le
```

Two edges per region (an edge gives out before the listener hosts):

```sh
scripts/linode-small-mesh-deploy.py restart $(scripts/compare/scale-run.py settings --edges-per-region 2)
scripts/compare/scale-run.py plan                                     # from the lab
scripts/compare/scale-run.py run --config pcm-1 --name e2-pcm-udp --hls-share 0 \
  --steps 100,1000,3000,4000,5000,6000,7000
```

Curve 1 (four edges per region):

```sh
scripts/linode-small-mesh-deploy.py restart $(scripts/compare/scale-run.py settings)
scripts/compare/scale-run.py run --config pcm-1 --name c1-pcm-udp --hls-share 0 \
  --steps 100,1000,4000,6000,8000
scripts/compare/scale-run.py run --config pcm-1 --name c1-pcm-mixed --force
scripts/compare/scale-run.py run --config opus-1 --name c1-opus1-mixed --force
scripts/compare/scale-run.py run --config opus-2.5 --name c1-opus25-mixed --force
scripts/compare/scale-run.py run --config opus-2.5 --name c1-opus25-udp --hls-share 0 \
  --steps 100,1000,5000,10000,15000,20000
```

`--force` runs steps that the plan marks as over the listener hosts. The
monitor still stops a step when a listener host saturates, and the ramp
ends there.

Second curve (eight London edges):

```sh
scripts/linode-small-mesh-deploy.py restart $(scripts/compare/scale-run.py settings --second-curve)
scripts/compare/scale-run.py run --config opus-2.5 --name c2-opus25-udp --hls-share 0 \
  --steps 100,1000,5000,10000,15000,20000
scripts/compare/scale-run.py run --config opus-2.5 --name c2-opus25-mixed --force
```

End of the run:

```sh
scripts/compare/scale-run.py stop
scripts/linode-small-mesh-lab.py down
# then check that no instance, firewall or VLAN with the run id is left
```

Each run's results go to `target/compare/scale-<name>/`:

- `plan.json`: allocation, capacity and cost
- `events.jsonl`: the monitor's lines and the stops
- `steps/<step>/<node>/`: the sampler, needletail-load's `udp/`
  (intervals, summary, the sampled listeners' arrivals) and the readers'
  condensed reports
- `steps/<step>/counters.json`: the edges' client UDP counters for the step
- `steps/<step>/step.json`
- `scale.json` and `summary.md`: per step and region, the lanes' p50 and
  p99, missing parts, time to first part, the tiers' CPU and Mbit/s out,
  UDP listeners per edge, public transfer, and what gave out first

`scale-run.py analyse <name>` rebuilds `scale.json` and `summary.md` from
the files.

## Limits of the tools

- **The LL-HLS figures are per reader.** ntcmp keeps a distribution per
  reader and no histogram. A region's p50 is the parts-weighted median of
  the readers' medians, and its p99 is the parts-weighted p99 of their
  p99s, which is an upper bound. The UDP figures merge exactly when every
  needletail-load summary carries its histogram buckets. When one does
  not, the worst host bounds the tail, and the report says so (`"merge"`).
- **ntcmp keeps every reader's latency values in memory**, one float per
  part per reader. Each step runs a fresh process, which keeps that
  memory bounded at these step lengths, but an hour-long crowd would not
  fit.
- **UDP latency is publication to arrival**, the part's publish time as
  the client reads it. LL-HLS latency is mouth to ear. The view hosts'
  full probes, run with the referee's tools, give mouth to ear for both
  lanes.
- **The Opus readers' indexes number part N as epoch N of the loop with
  offset 0**, as the referee's `nt-popus-120s` does. If the AEP1 Opus lane
  shifts by the encoder's look-ahead, every Opus mouth-to-ear figure moves
  by that constant: 1 ms for Opus Custom, 2.5 ms for standard Opus.
