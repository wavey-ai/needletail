# The large deployed mesh: audio, simulated listeners

On 22 November the owner asked to "test on a larger deployed mesh", "with
simulated users too - as many as we can simulate. lets stick to audio for
now", and then raised the shared Linode budget of the Needletail work to
$100 (the large-mesh run included; the dev box is not counted). The run
also has to show that failover of edges, distributors and the ingress
loses no listener, that caches redistribute content under load without
overload, and that edges and distribution nodes scale up and down without
interrupting listeners: the phases below. The topology starts from the referee's plan
(needletail-compare `target/compare/scale-plan.md`) and is grown to as many
listener hosts as that budget buys, with enough edges in each region that
on the second curve the edges, not the listener hosts, give out first. The
topology file is `deploy/low-latency/topology-scale.json`, generated by
`scripts/scale_topology.py`. The run's driver is `scripts/compare/scale-run.py`.

**This run: six billed hours, without Sydney** (agreed with the referee, 22
November). 69 nodes at $7.884 an hour; the watchdog is armed for 6.0 hours
from the lab's creation, and the lab tool's watchdog starts `down` 5
minutes before that. A refused core stops the run with no retry, and
nothing is added past the schedule. The run starts with two windows run
with the referee's own tools, before curve 1: **(A) the wave 7 audio
headline** and **(B) 4K with GSO** (see "The two windows before curve 1").
No ramp step runs the joins phase (shed, see "Cost and duration"). The
whole schedule, from `up` to `down`, is the `schedule` of
`deploy/low-latency/phases-scale.json`, and `scale-run.py plan
--topology-only --json` reports it (`schedule.total_min`): 341.3 minutes,
against a limit of 345 (6 hours less the 5 minutes and a margin of 10
before the watchdog's `down`). See "Cost and duration".

## What is measured

- **Audio only.** Three lanes, one per ramp, all from the AEP1 sender on
  lon-src through the contributor:
  - `pcm-1`: 1 ms 24-bit PCM, stream 4001
  - `opus-1`: 1 ms Opus Custom at 320 kb/s, stream 3001
  - `opus-2.5`: standard 2.5 ms Opus, stream 3001
- **Listener steps:** 100, 1,000, 5,000, 10,000, 20,000 and up (about
  28,000 UDP listeners of 1 ms PCM fill the second curve's edges). Each
  step is spread over the regions in proportion to their edges, and over a
  region's listener hosts in proportion to their vCPUs. Half of each step
  listens over UDP and half over LL-HLS (`--hls-share 0` for UDP alone):
  - **UDP:** `needletail-load` from av-mesh `low-latency` (branch `scale-load`
    merged at d0d9ee4, on the wave 7 client lanes). Each
    listener has its own socket and subscription, with the real FEC and
    compact decode, NACKs and loss accounting. Results are sampled rather
    than logged, as histograms of publication-to-arrival time and of time
    to first part.
  - **LL-HLS:** `ntcmp hls --count` with 40 part requests in flight over
    HTTP/3. Readers are pinned evenly to the region's edges (`cache=`), or
    left to the region's steering (`--hls-steering steered`). ntcmp
    measures mouth to ear: the age of each part against the source's spoken
    time.
- **At every step**, per region: p50 and p99 of each lane, the share of
  parts missing, and the time to first part or first audio; from each
  view host, the time to first audio of new listeners (the joins phase,
  below, in the steps that run it), and LiveKit's figures in the same
  step. Also UDP
  listeners per edge, plus CPU, bytes and packets out of every node of
  every tier, from a 1 s sampler on all nodes. That data shows whether the
  contributor and forwarder tiers stay flat as listeners grow.
- **Which tier gave out first, and when:** an edge, a forwarder, the
  contributor or a listener host. A node gives out when its CPU stays at
  90% or more for 3 s, or its bytes out stay at 90% of its plan's outbound
  cap. An edge also gives out when its UDP listeners miss more than 1% of
  an interval's parts, counted only from the listener hosts that kept up
  in that interval. A listener host gives out on CPU or UDP receive-buffer
  drops held for 3 s, or on needletail-load's own verdict held for 3
  intervals (host CPU or one busy core, worker busy time, late timers,
  receive lag, socket drops, or drops before the sockets at the NIC and
  backlog). When a listener host saturates, the step is stopped at once.
  From then on the figures would describe the load host, not the mesh.
- **The ramp ends** at the first step where something gave out
  (`--keep-going` runs past it).
- **Moving the curve out:** two edges per region, then five (curve 1),
  then ten (curve 2). Edges are taken in and out of service with the
  deploy's `start --nodes` and `stop --nodes`, which restart nothing else
  (see "Joining without a restart"), so no instance is created mid-run
  except by the autoscaler, and only an autoscale slot.
- **Failover, redistribution and autoscaling:** the phases of
  `phases-scale.json`, each group one held step of curve 1 (below).
- **Control: self-hosted LiveKit in the same steps.** Each step also runs
  the referee's lkregion lane (one LiveKit SFU in each region, `<r>-lk`,
  already in the topology) at its own capacity: the step's listeners of
  the region up to 450 per SFU (about what a 2-vCPU SFU carried on wave 2;
  `--lk-per-sfu`, 0 to leave it out). The crowd runs on the region's
  listener hosts beside Needletail's, spread by vCPU and counted in their
  planned load, for the step's ramp and hold; the publishers (one per SFU,
  on lon-src) run for the whole ramp. The commands are the referee's own:
  `scale-run.py` imports needletail-compare's
  `scripts/compare/compare-audio.py` (branch compare-cloudflare;
  `NEEDLETAIL_COMPARE_ROOT`, default `../needletail-compare`) and runs its
  `Audio.probe("lkregion", ...)` (`ntcmp-lk sub --count N`, joining over
  at most 10 s) and the publishers' command line of its `Audio.start`
  (mirrored, not called: `Audio.start` also restarts the contributor and
  starts Needletail's producers, which this run's sender replaces). Each
  step logs the crowd per region (`livekit_by_site` in events.jsonl), writes
  its records in the referee's layout (`compare/RUN-STEP/audio.CONFIG.scale.
  STEP/HOST/lkregion.probe-NNN.json` and meta.json) and summarises them with
  the referee's `compare-audio-analyse.py`: per region the subscribers
  planned and reporting, with audio, unit latency p50 and p99, units
  missing, first audio, and the SFU's CPU from the sampler. An SFU that
  saturates is LiveKit's limit, never a tier of the mesh giving out. The
  lanes map to the referee's configurations: pcm-1 to `pcm` (LiveKit's PCM
  in 2.5 ms data packets), opus-1 to `opus-2.5` with Needletail's 1 ms Opus
  Custom (the referee's smallest-frame session), opus-2.5 to `opus-2.5`.
- **Time to first audio at scale: the joins phase at representative
  steps.** In this run: c1-pcm-udp's first step (100) and 10,000, and the
  top of curve 1 (c1-opus25-udp's 40,000), `--joins-at`; window A runs the
  referee's own joins in full. From the end of a step's ramp, on each
  region's view host, the
  referee's joins phase runs on the running stream under the step's load:
  20 new listeners of each lane, one after another (the lanes side by
  side), each a new process timed from its start to its first audio unit
  (ntcmp's `first_audio_ms`: connection, signalling or entry assignment,
  then the first unit). The lanes are the referee's for the step's
  configuration: lkregion, Needletail's UDP client lane nt-uc (the
  scoreboard's), and its LL-HLS readers (`nt-pcm`, `-w`, `-h3`, or
  `nt-popus...`). It is the referee's implementation, invoked, not copied:
  `Audio.joins(20, 5)` of the imported compare-audio.py, which writes
  `LANE.join-NN.json` and meta.json under `compare/RUN-STEP/audio.CONFIG.
  joins.20/VIEW-HOST/`; the referee's analysis summarises them into the
  step (`joins`: per view host and lane, joins with audio, first audio p50,
  p95, max), and its `scoreboard.py` reads them as its time-to-first-audio
  row (Needletail nt-uc against LiveKit lkregion; a test runs the
  scoreboard on such records). A step that runs it holds at least 140 s
  (20 joins x 6.5 s, the 5 s probe, the 0.5 s pause and about a second to
  start, + 10 s), instead of 60 s, so that every join meets the step's
  listeners; each step logs the joins' start and end and whether they
  ended inside the hold (`within_hold`). That length is an estimate (the
  referee's own bound for the same call is 190 s), so a join whose probe
  did not run wholly inside the hold (its `started_unix_ms` + 5 s past the
  hold's end, or past the moment a saturated step's listeners were
  stopped) is set aside before the referee's analysis reads the records
  (to `VIEW-HOST/past-the-hold/`), counted per view host and lane (`late`
  in the step's `joins`, the summary's last column, `joins_past_the_hold`
  in events.jsonl) and never scored: it met a mesh and an SFU whose crowd
  had stopped. `--joins 0` leaves it out,
  `--joins-from N` runs it only in steps of N listeners or more,
  `--joins-at 100,10000` only in the steps named (as asked in `--steps`;
  it overrides `--joins-from`). Not in a
  phase run unless asked (`--joins 20`), and never when the phase plan
  faults a region's dist-a: the referee's Needletail probes enter there
  (F5, F6 and F8 fault lon-dist-a).

## Joining without a restart

Every peer list is rendered from the lab's roster, not from the nodes in
service: every edge and distributor, the standby edges (created, held out
of service) and the autoscale slots, created or not. The distributors'
cache-mesh `--peer` lists, every node's `--telemetry-peer` list and the
fast-plane feed (each edge's rank, the forwarders' lease children) name
them all from the first push. A node held out or not created yet is a peer
that is down: telemetry peers are retried every second and the cache mesh
ignores a peer not heard within 2 s. So every node's arguments are the same
whichever of the roster serve (a test renders the first curve before and
after a standby edge, an edge slot and both forwarder slots start: every
other node's arguments and the compiled relay DAG are byte for byte the
same), and:

- `linode-small-mesh-deploy.py start --nodes a,b` takes held-out nodes into
  service and starts them, and touches nothing else;
- `stop --nodes a,b` holds them out and stops them, and touches nothing
  else;
- `push --nodes a,b` bootstraps, installs and starts a node just created
  (it keeps the routes of the last whole push).

A forwarder slot (dist-c) is never a compiled relay child: a child of its
own would take an index and move the relay ports of every child after it.
It is rendered as a cache-mesh distributor of its site's edges, fed as a
fast-plane lease child of the site's dist-a and dist-b. That av-mesh runs a
distributor fed that way is Phase AM item 12 (below).

A new copy's warm-up is a length of time, not a count of parts:
`cache_mesh_warm_ms` 4,500 in the scale settings (the client's 1 s silence
detection + the 3 s move bound of F2 + 0.5 s), rendered as parts of the
shortest audio part (4,500 at 1 ms; Opus of 2.5 ms gets 11.25 s). 300 parts
would be 0.3 s at 1 ms parts, shorter than a move.

Prices were read from `GET /linode/types` on 22 November: $0.054, $0.108
and $0.216 an hour for g6-dedicated-2, -4 and -8, with no region override
in gb-lon, us-east, ap-northeast or ap-southeast. Outbound caps are 4, 5
and 6 Gbit/s. Public transfer past the account's pool costs $0.005/GB
(`GET /network-transfer/prices`). `scripts/linode-small-mesh-lab.py check
--topology deploy/low-latency/topology-scale.json` re-reads all of this
from the API's public endpoints, plus each region's capacity for each
plan. It needs no token and creates nothing.

**Capacity and account limits.** `GET /regions/<id>/availability` on 22
November (re-read for this sizing): g6-dedicated-2, -4 and -8 available in
gb-lon, us-east and ap-northeast; in ap-southeast g6-dedicated-2 and -4
only. Sydney's listener hosts are therefore sixteen g6-dedicated-4, the
same 64 vCPUs as the other regions' eight g6-dedicated-8 at the same
price. The availability endpoint has been wrong for Sydney before, which
is why Sydney is optional and created first. What this account may create
in all is not in a public endpoint and was not checked: the largest lab
so far had 33 nodes, and this one has 69 (40 g6-dedicated-2, 5
g6-dedicated-4 and 24 g6-dedicated-8: 292 dedicated vCPUs) or, with
Sydney, 99 (53, 22 and 24: 386 vCPUs). Confirm it before `up` if you can
(Linode support raises the limits on request). `up` does not count on it:
it creates in stages, and each stage stops at its first refusal (the
creates already under way, at most three, finish and are recorded).

1. **Sydney's core** (`--optional-sites sydney`): its forwarders, edges,
   view host and SFU, 14 nodes. A refusal deletes what of Sydney was
   created and goes on without it.
2. **The core**: the contributor, the source, the forwarders, the edges
   (the standby ones too), the view hosts, the LiveKit SFUs and their
   Redis: 45 nodes, $2.70 an hour (59 and $3.51 with Sydney's). Without it
   the lab is of no use, so a refusal deletes the partial lab and prints
   what it billed: every instance created bills an hour, so a refused core
   costs at most $2.70 ($3.51 with Sydney). An account refusal (the limit
   of instances or of dedicated vCPUs) first deletes Sydney's nodes, which
   count against the same limit, and tries the rest of the core again.
3. **The listener hosts**, in two batches (`--listener-batches 2`), each
   with half of every region's (the first batch: lon-, new-, tok-load-1 to
   4 and syd-load-1 to 8). A refusal here keeps the lab. An account
   refusal stops every later create; a capacity refusal stops only that
   region's plan; a transient one only that host. The hosts not created are in the lab state's
   `listeners_missing`, with the refusal; `status` lists them, and `grow`
   creates them once the account allows. A Sydney left with no listener
   host is deleted (its edges would carry no one).

Refusals are told apart by the API's message (`refusal_kind` in the lab
tool): no capacity ("capacity", "not available in this region", "out of
stock", ...: tried first, so a capacity message that suggests a support
ticket is still capacity, unless it names the account's limit), an
account limit ("Account Limit reached. Please open a support ticket ...",
"maximum number of", "cpu limit", ...; "quota", "support ticket" or
"request an increase" only with "limit" or "account" beside them) or a
transient error (HTTP 408, 429, 5xx, busy, timed out, once `create_node`'s
retries are spent). A transient refusal stops nothing: its node is tried
once more at the end of its stage (after 30 s), and only a second refusal
counts, so one flaky create does not roll back a core that costs $2.70. The exact text Linode returns at this account's limit
has not been seen: a refusal of no known kind stops creation as an account
refusal does, and is recorded as "other". Every refusal is kept in the
lab state's `refusals`.

**A lab short of listener hosts.** When the lab state names
`listeners_missing`, `scale-run.py plan` and `run` fit every step to the
listener hosts that exist rather than refusing it: each region takes at
most what its hosts drive within the plan's headroom (`fit_caps`, from the
per-host capacity and the step's LL-HLS share), the other regions keep
their share, a step is named by the listeners it runs
(`requested` is what was asked), and a step that would add no listener is
dropped. A region left with no listener host at all (a capacity
refusal of Tokyo's g6-dedicated-8 in the first batch blocks its plan in
the second too) keeps its share of every step, run by nobody: the other
regions are never given it, so their edges carry what they carry on the
whole lab (`idle_regions` in plan.json and each step, printed and logged).
Its edges, SFU and view host are idle and billed, and it runs no joins;
`up` names it, and `grow` creates its hosts once the account allows (or
`down` and `up --without-sites` it). The fit is printed, logged (`fit` in
events.jsonl) and kept in plan.json. `--force` does not turn it off, so every command below,
`--force` or not, is fitted on a short lab: `--force` lets a step over the
headroom run, and the fit then fills each region's hosts as far over the
headroom as the whole lab would (the 106% step stays at 106%, not twice
that on half the hosts; `fit_load_limit` in the step). `--no-fit` puts
each region's share on the hosts it has, unfitted (the monitor still stops
a step whose listener host saturates); `--fit-hosts` fits a whole lab to
the headroom too. The schedule below then ends early: the run costs the
hours it lasts.

**Listeners on the VLAN.** The listener and view hosts are on their
region's VLAN (.100 the view host, .101 up the listener hosts), and every
listener reads the edges there (`playback_address=vlan`). Linode does not
bill VLAN traffic, but it bills public egress. One 1 ms PCM UDP listener
is about 5.6 Mbit/s on the wire, so 28,000 of them are about 157 Gbit/s:
some 70 TB an hour, or about $350 an hour past the pool. `scale-run.py
run` refuses to start when a listener host or an edge has no VLAN address,
or when the deploy's `playback_address` is not `vlan`. Public traffic that
remains: the long-haul forwarder links (one stream to six remote
forwarders, a few Mbit/s), and the LiveKit crowd, which the SFUs send to
their public address (LiveKit advertises it; the referee's installer sets
`node_ip` to it): about 2.6 Mbit/s a subscriber of PCM and 0.42 of Opus,
1,800 subscribers at most (1,350 without Sydney). `plan` prices it
(`livekit_public_gb`, and the schedule's sum of it): over this run's
schedule (no Sydney, joins at three steps) 0.75 TB, $3.77 were none of it
in the account's transfer pool. The referee's bound of about $9.86 (1.97
TB) was for Sydney and the joins in every step (without Sydney, with the
joins in every step: 1.55 TB, $7.73). The two windows add
public traffic of their own: (A) a few listeners a region, about 10 GB
at most; (B) 4K on the public path, about 45 GB at most (four readers a region
at each rung for 40 s, and the forwarders' long haul), $0.23 past the
pool. The lab's own instances add to the pool for the hours they live;
how much of this month's pool is used was not checked. Every step
reports the public bytes that left each node (`public_transfer_gb`).

Edges 6 to 10 of every region are created with the lab but held out of the
first curve. The topology's `standby_edges`, passed to the deploy as the
setting of the same name, runs no service on them; every peer list names
them all the same (a peer that is down, see "Joining without a restart"),
and nothing steers to them, for they advertise nothing. The second curve
starts them with `start --nodes`, which restarts no other node. This
avoids creating instances in the middle of a run.

Sydney is optional. `up --optional-sites sydney` creates Sydney's core (14
nodes) first and its 16 listener hosts with the other regions'. If its
core cannot be created, if the account refuses the core, or if none of its
listener hosts is created, `up` deletes Sydney's nodes and goes on without
it, which saves $2.538 an hour. Every tool works without it.
`--without-sites sydney` leaves Sydney out from the start.

## The two windows before curve 1

The run starts with two windows, each run with the referee's own tools
(needletail-compare, branch compare-cloudflare, its checkout and builds)
on this lab: `NEEDLETAIL_LINODE_LAB_STATE` points them at this lab's state,
and `COMPARE_SITES=lon,new,tok` at its three regions (the referee's tools
would otherwise look for Sydney's nodes). Both run on one deploy, pushed by
`up`'s bring-up: **one edge a region in service, the listeners on the
public path** (`playback_address` public), GSO on (`relay_video_gso`, the
default, set explicitly), and no simulated listener. Public, because the
referee's impairment (`compare-netem`, `NETEM_DEV` eth0) acts on the view
host's public interface: on the VLAN the Needletail lanes would escape the
loss that LiveKit's lanes get. This lab has no `<r>-nano` hosts (the
referee's labs do): its steps name them, and they show as host errors in
each step's meta; the view hosts' probes, which its scoreboard prefers,
run.

- **(A) The wave 7 audio headline, 23 minutes.** Two sessions of the
  referee's `compare-audio.py`, as its scoreboard reads them
  (`--sessions W,W-std`): `scale-w7`, configurations opus-2.5 with 1 ms
  Opus Custom (`--nt-opus-samples 48`: Needletail 1 ms against LiveKit's
  2.5 ms, the smallest frame each forwards) and pcm (1 ms PCM, 48 samples,
  against LiveKit's 2.5 ms PCM data packets), about 15 minutes; then
  `scale-w7-std`, opus-2.5 with standard 2.5 ms Opus (`--nt-opus-samples
  120`), about 7.5 minutes. Lanes: lkregion (the self-hosted SFU of each
  region), Needletail's UDP lane nt-uc and its LL-HLS readers (nt-popus or
  nt-pcm, `-w`, `-h3`), in the same session. Phases: baseline (clean
  network, 60 s), the referee's joins (20 new listeners a lane on each view
  host), and impair: 1% loss, 5% loss and bursts (Gilbert-Elliott, 4.8%),
  and the harness's 100 ms delay, which its impair phase always runs (35 s
  each). The time is from the referee's own sessions: `waudio6` (opus-2.5
  and pcm, baseline and impair) took 8.9 minutes and `waudio6-std` 4.1;
  the joins add about 2.5 minutes a configuration (20 x 6.5 s, the
  referee's bound 190 s). `compare-audio.py` restarts the contributor (it
  finds the referee's own deploy settings' `daw_hls_formats` equal to
  `flac,opus,pcm`, the scale settings' value too; check it first: were
  they different, it would redeploy the contributor with the referee's
  settings) and runs its own AEP1 sender on lon-src, stopped at its end.
- **(B) 4K with GSO, 8 minutes.** The referee's `compare-run.py --lanes nt
  --phases throughput` (its `video-w5-gso` and `video-w6` sweeps): the
  contributor, one edge a region and each region's view host on the public
  path, rungs 2160p25, 40, 80, 160 and 240 of the wave 6 encodes
  (`clips6-full`), 40 s a rung, about 80 s with the source's restart and
  the fetch (`video-w5-gso`: seven rungs in 9.4 minutes; `video-w6`: three
  in 3.7). The sweep ends early after two rungs that reach no region
  whole. The clips (6.4 GB) are copied to lon-src during the bring-up.

Then the deploy is restarted onto the VLAN (`scale-run.py settings`, which
sets `playback_address` back to vlan; `scale-run.py run` refuses anything
else) for the ramps.

## Expectations (to be confirmed by the run)

- **At 1 ms, the listener hosts carry about 32,000 UDP listeners.**
  needletail-load's bench (part L, 22 Nov) measured 3.96 µs of CPU per
  datagram, kernel included, with nothing missing. Adding about 1.5 µs of
  NIC receive softirq gives 5.5 µs, or 0.0055 vCPU per listener of a
  1,000-part stream: about 1,000 listeners per g6-dedicated-8 within 70%
  busy, 8,000 per region of 64 vCPUs, about 32,000 in four regions (24,000
  without Sydney). At 2.5 ms (opus-2.5, 400 parts a second) about 2.5
  times as many. The cost is per datagram because each listener has its
  own socket, so the spec's 5,000 per g6-dedicated-4 at 1 ms is out of
  reach. Memory is about 0.44 MB per listener and does not bind: the
  1,000 listeners of a g6-dedicated-8 take about 0.44 GB of its 16 GB.
- **An edge sends at most 4 Gbit/s,** about 700 PCM listeners at 1 ms or
  about 3,000 at 1 ms Opus. Ten edges a region send to about 7,000 PCM
  listeners at 1 ms, below the region's 8,000: on the second curve the
  edges give out first (at about 28,000 listeners in all, the listener
  hosts near 60% busy). Five edges a region (curve 1) give out at about
  14,000, two (`settings --edges-per-region 2`) at about 5,600; without
  Sydney (this run) about 21,000, 10,500 and 4,200, the 90% give-out rule
  a little before. With
  opus-2.5 an edge sends 0.6 Mbit/s a listener, so its CPU, not its NIC,
  is what that ramp tests; the listener hosts drive about 80,000 of them.
- **LL-HLS at 1 ms parts is the load that runs out first on the listener
  hosts.** Each reader asks for 1,000 parts a second. The plan's planning
  figure is 0.03 vCPU of a listener host per reader, so a g6-dedicated-8
  carries about 190 readers within 70% busy, about 6,000 in all. For
  pcm-1, the plan marks the half LL-HLS 10,000 step as over the listener
  hosts. That is why the UDP-only ramps are in the schedule.
  `--hls-cap-per-host N` keeps LL-HLS at what the hosts carry and sends
  the rest over UDP (marked "clipped" in the plan).
- **The contributor and forwarder tiers should stay flat:** one stream in,
  and one feed per edge (ten a forwarder on curve 2) and per remote
  forwarder.
- **Every step measures what a listener cost its host.** It reports
  needletail-load's and ntcmp's own CPU, as the sampler reads it per
  process, and peak memory, both per listener, as the median over the
  listener hosts. UDP and LL-HLS each get 0.0015 vCPU of NIC softirq
  added, which a process's own CPU time does not include. The run writes
  these to `cost.json`, in the shape that `--cost FILE` takes, for
  `plan` and `run` to use. Pass `--udp-mbps` once a step has measured
  the wire rate. One measured figure is still missing: what an LL-HLS
  reader costs (the plan assumes 0.03 vCPU at 1 ms parts, part L's
  unmeasured estimate for HTTP/2; HTTP/3 may cost more). The calibration
  ramp measures it before the large steps.

## Limits of the tools

- **The LL-HLS figures are per reader.** ntcmp keeps a distribution per
  reader and no histogram. A region's p50 is the parts-weighted median of
  the readers' medians, and its p99 is the parts-weighted p99 of their
  p99s, which is an upper bound. The UDP figures merge exactly when every
  needletail-load summary carries its histogram buckets. When one does
  not, the worst host bounds the tail, and the report says so (`"merge"`).
- **ntcmp keeps every reader's latency values in memory**, one float per
  part per reader, and writes one report per reader. Each step runs a
  fresh process, which bounds that memory at these step lengths (about 3
  MB per reader for a 90 s step), but an hour-long crowd would not fit.
  The summary shows ntcmp's peak MB per reader.
- **The joins enter over the public network.** The referee's probes take
  each region's dist-a by its public address (compare-audio.py), as in its
  own sessions; the media then comes from the edge that the entry assigns,
  on the VLAN (`playback_address=vlan`). Twenty joins of 5 s a lane is a
  few hundred MB a step. A join's time includes its process's start on
  the view host, as the referee's does.
- **The joins' length is an estimate** (6.5 s a join; the referee bounds
  it at 9.5): `within_hold` in each step's record says whether they ended
  before the step's listeners did. A join that ran past it met a mesh
  that was emptying, and is set aside unscored (`late`): if the estimate
  is short, the last joins of each lane are lost, not mis-scored. Holding
  for the referee's 190 s instead adds 50 s to each of the three steps
  that run the joins (2.5 minutes; the schedule has 3.9 to spare).
- **The LiveKit crowd's cost per subscriber is a planning figure** (0.008
  vCPU at 400 units a second, `lk_vcpu` 0.02 at 1,000): the calibration
  measures it from ntcmp-lk's CPU in the sampler and writes it to
  cost.json. Its traffic is public (the SFUs advertise their public
  address), priced in `plan` as transfer.
- **UDP latency is publication to arrival**, the part's publish time as
  the client reads it. LL-HLS latency is mouth to ear. The view hosts'
  full probes, run with the referee's tools, give mouth to ear for both
  lanes.
- **The Opus readers' indexes number part N as epoch N of the loop with
  offset 0**, as the referee's `nt-popus-120s` does. If the AEP1 Opus lane
  shifts by the encoder's look-ahead, every Opus mouth-to-ear figure moves
  by that constant: 1 ms for Opus Custom, 2.5 ms for standard Opus.

## Failover, redistribution and autoscaling

`deploy/low-latency/phases-scale.json` is the plan, `scale-run.py run
--phase-plan` the driver. Each group is one held step of curve 1 (6,000
listeners, 5% of them LL-HLS; A: 6,000 UDP listeners at its peak).
Listener figures here are listeners; with two-path listeners (B) the edges'
`client_udp.subscribers` and `load_permille` count legs, two a listener.
Each scenario: 60 s of baseline, its probes from 45 s before the fault, the
fault, at least 90 s of observation (longer when its change commands'
bounds are), the restore, its probes held on after (below), 30 s to settle;
the next scenario's baseline shows whether the mesh came back.

**One scenario, one verdict (C CL7).** Every scenario has probes of its
own: on each view host and probed config the referee's nt-uc probe
(`needletail-udp-client --entry https://<site's dist-a>:19444 --stream S
--arrivals A --write-parts P --duration-seconds D`), started 45 s before its
first action; then `ntcmp udp-ingest` on the view host, the files fetched,
and the referee's `change-window.py --frames ... --marks
scenarios/NAME/marks.txt` on the dev box with that scenario's marks only.
The referee's rule of 23 November scores a change against the same probe's
own no-change windows (at least 120 of the change's length plus 7 s), so a
probe runs on after the end of its change's bound for twice that bound and
about 140 s more, and 360 s in all at least (`probe_tail_s`); a change that
outlives its probes is unscored ("null too short"), never passed. Every
verdict carries `null_fail_rate` where change-window.py could measure it:
it scores each no-change window against the others more than the window's
length + 1 s away and skips one with fewer than 120 of them, yet divides by
every window, so on fewer than 121 + 2 x (length + 1) windows (every
long change of these plans) its rate is 0.0 by construction; the record
then says "not measured" (`null_fail_rate_unmeasured`) and the scenario's
`null_fail_rate_max` leaves it out. Which probes were meant to run is
written before they start (`probes-expected.json`): a view host or config
whose probe never started or never came back fails "probe: pass on every
lane and view host", naming it. `--probe-configs opus-1,pcm-1` publishes
and probes more than the run's config (the crowd stays on the run's); a
plan's `defaults.probe_configs` sets it.

**Two paths before a change (B's seam).** Before each scenario's probes the
runner waits, 20 s at most, until every listener host's newest interval
shows no listener on one path (`paths.one == 0`), so that one change's
exposure is not charged to the next. The probes then start that much later
and end on time. A timeout fails "crowd on two paths before the change"; a
load tool without `paths` leaves it open; a one-path crowd (`--load-args
"--paths 1"`) has no such criterion. The edges'
`client_udp.assignment_confirms_limited` must not rise (open until the edges
give it).

**Marks and windows.** A change command's own `start`/`end` lines are the
scenario's marks; a plain kind's mark starts when its signal or request
went and ends when its wait did (a drain at `drain_end`, a kill or stop
when its restore has the node back and ready, a crash when the node has a
new boot id and is ready, a join at `first_serving`). A label that a dead
command left open is closed when it was reaped, with an `aborted` event.
A scenario's windows are its marks widened as the referee widens them (2 s
before, 5 s after): `before` from its slot's start, `during`, `after` to
its slot's end. A scenario with no marks did not run and fails "the
scenario ran". A scenario whose probes would start more than 10 s late (a
change before it ran past its bound) is skipped and logged, never started.

**Change kinds** (one blocking command each, never cut: once the step has
stopped a change under way is waited for up to its bound and 60 s more, and
only then sent SIGINT; one that SIGINT does not end logs the `--resume` it
owes, `resume_owed`; a restore, A's `return` among them, is never stopped by
the step, only once past its own bound, loudly, `restore_unfinished`; a
controller gets SIGINT): `roll` (`deploy roll TIER --force`, the same build;
`dry`: `--dry-marks`), `upgrade` (`deploy roll TIER` with the roots of
`--next-av-mesh-root`/`--next-av-contrib-root`, or the run's own for
`first`; a run with a roll or an upgrade refuses to start unless
`NEEDLETAIL_AV_MESH_ROOT` and `NEEDLETAIL_AV_CONTRIB_ROOT` name the run's
build), `restart` (`deploy restart --nodes`), `replace` (`deploy replace
--nodes T --mode M` with the run's budget; a rebuilt node's samplers are
started again after it), `scale` (`linode-autoscale.py
scale up|down --site S --tier T`), `change` (workstream A's `deploy kill
--node X` and `return --node X`; its kill's restore is its return), and
`autoscale` (`linode-autoscale.py run --until join|stopped` for the rest of
the observation; `background` lets a plain fault run beside it). The
engine's timeouts go to each deploy command and set its bound
(`action_bound_s`). An action may wait `"after": {"event": E, "node": N,
"label": L, "timeout_s": T}` in place of `at_s` (an event of the run's log:
`drain_end`, `first_serving`, `first_advertised`, a mark's `end` with its
label, a controller's line): on a timeout its scenario's later actions are
skipped, its restores still run, and "event E within T s" fails. Kill,
crash, stop and start act on a node of any role through its role's unit
(`needletail-contrib` for a contributor).

- **Faults.** A kill is SIGKILL and then the unit stopped: node loss (with
  `Restart=always` alone the node would be back in 2 s). The 2 s restart
  is its own scenario (F2r, a crash). A drain is POST
  /api/control/drain-node naming the node. At most 2 of a region's 5
  serving edges at once: 5 of 5 would be the loss of the region. A
  drained node advertises that it drains at once and sends its first
  listeners away 100 ms later (av-mesh cdfeb5c), so that the entry points
  never send the movers of one drained edge to another drained at the
  same moment (before: 3-17 of 55-70 movers moved twice in R5, time to
  move p99 110-118 ms; after: none, 16-21 ms).
- **Entry points not under fault.** The LL-HLS readers enter, and the
  stream's liveness is read, at nodes no phase faults (London's entry is
  lon-edge-5 in group F, since F5, F6 and F8 fault both London forwarders).
- **Readers that move.** The LL-HLS listeners of a phase run are
  needletail-reader crowds with `--steering` and several entry points: they
  move when their cache fails or drains, and record each move and its
  reason. ntcmp's chase window does not re-steer, and no Go toolchain was
  on the dev box to change it.
- **Pass criteria of C CL7, every scenario.** The referee's verdict on
  every probe and label ("probe: pass on every lane and view host"; open
  when it was not scored). The crowd, from needletail-load's v2 fields (B
  Y4; each open, never passed, until the tool gives it):
  `listeners_interrupted` 0 in the window, `listeners_with_missing` 0 in
  and after it, `gaps`, `resets`, `never_resumed`, `listeners_without_parts`
  0, the longest silence inside at most the baseline's plus one unit, the
  latency p99 of the window's buckets at most the baseline's plus 1 ms, and
  the longest single-path span 0 for a planned change (100 ms for the loss
  of an edge: B's 80 ms and a round trip; a plan's `expect.single_path_ms`
  otherwise). The steered readers: no stall in the window. The nodes the

  action under way. An `add` is put back by stopping the slot (`down`
  deletes it); a `remove` needs a drain of the slot earlier in its

- **The controller.** `scripts/linode-autoscale.py`, on the operator host
  (it needs the Linode token, which never goes onto a mesh node): a
  control-plane loop that decides from the mesh's own advertisement
  (/api/assignment), with no human in the loop. The record of a run says
  that the controller ran on the operator host; the in-mesh
  edge_lifecycle stays off (it can close an edge with UDP listeners). It
  creates and deletes only autoscale slots of the recorded lab, through the
  lab tool; drains before it deletes (resume and no delete when the drain
  does not reach 0 in 60 s); scales up at a mean of 700 permille for 3
  samples, at one edge of 850 or more with the mean at 600 or more for 3
  samples (wave 9 C3: a ramp fills the edges unevenly) when that edge's UDP
  listeners, at the region's cost of a listener, account for its load over
  800 (wave 9 review: an edge hot from its LL-HLS readers' CPU is not cooled
  by a new edge), or refusals in 2,
  down when the load with one edge fewer stays at or under 450 for 300 s; one region at a time per 60 s, never an add and a
  remove within 5 min; at most 2 nodes an action; joining nodes counted;
  standby edges first, then drained slots inside their paid hour (the warm
  pool), then a create; a warm slot is deleted 5 min before its next billed
  hour; every intent written to its state before it acts, so a restart
  picks up where it stopped. A forwarder slot counts as drained only when
  its lease children and cache-mesh children read 0 as well (AM item 12;
  until then it is resumed, never deleted). SIGINT or SIGTERM: it resumes
  every drain in flight, then exits. One controller per state file (a
  lock). In a phase run its state is `autoscale-state.json` in the step's
  directory (A1 hands its warm pool to A2), its output
  `scenarios/<scenario>/command-N.out` and `.err`, its exit code a
  `controller_exit` event; a controller is one blocking action, so A2's
  starts only once A1's has exited, and the step's end stops one still
  running with SIGINT.
- **The edges rebalance (wave 9 C1).** An edge over 800 permille
  (`--rebalance-target-permille`) sends some of its UDP listeners `MOVE
  rebalance`, paced over 2 s, a round every 3 s, when an edge of its region
  is under 650 (the target less `--client-udp-rebalance-margin-permille`
  150): never more than its own excess, nor more than the receivers' room up
  to 725 shared among the edges over the target by their excess (each
  receiver judged by the larger of its advertised load and its live
  subscribers at the region's cost of a listener, since the advertised load
  is a 10 s mean). The client asks the assignment again without the edge
  (wave 9 review: a rebalanced listener goes only to an edge that stays
  under 725 with it counted, the share of each by its room to 725) and
  resumes from its oldest hole. A listener admitted less than 10 s ago is
  not moved. An edge whose rounds did not lower its load (one load window
  after its last move, by less than half of what the moved listeners cost,
  allowing for listeners that came) pauses its rebalance for 60 s
  (`client_udp.rebalance_ineffective`).
  So a joined edge takes the excess that its region had before it joined,
  not only new arrivals. Rebalance moves are counted by reason in
  needletail-load and `client_udp.rebalance_moves` on the edge.
- **An edge's load (wave 9 C2).** load_permille is the largest of the bytes
  against the egress admission limit, the client datagrams against
  `client_udp_datagram_capacity`, the sessions against the session limit, and
  the process's CPU over its available CPUs (5 s mean, 1,000 at 85 %:
  `--cpu-load-full-percent`); `cpu_permille` beside it. The entry points'
  free capacity of an edge is the less of the bytes' headroom and the load's.
